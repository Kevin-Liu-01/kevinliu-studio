# Languages

The site speaks eight languages. English is the source and lives at the root, unchanged; each other language lives
under its code: `/es`, `/es/cards/turboslide`, `/es/battle/arena`, `/es/about.md`, `/es/llms.txt`.

| Code | `lang` | Translated by | Face |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| en | en | (source) | Katagami Sans, site subset |
| es | es | General Translation | + Katagami Sans, accented Latin |
| fr | fr | General Translation | + Katagami Sans, accented Latin |
| de | de | General Translation | + Katagami Sans, accented Latin |
| pt | pt-BR | General Translation | + Katagami Sans, accented Latin |
| ja | ja | General Translation | + Katagami CJK JP subset |
| ko | ko | General Translation | + Katagami CJK KR subset |
| zh | zh-Hans | General Translation | + Katagami CJK SC subset |

`src/i18n/locales.mjs` is the one list; routes, hreflang, the sitemap, the language key, `vercel.json`'s patterns and
the guard read it (`vercel.json` spells the codes out: change both together).

## How a translation is made

Nothing is translated at run time, and no key is needed to build: the translations are committed JSON.

1. `src/i18n/en/ui.json` holds the interface (labels, headings, page copy, the twins' sentences), written by hand.
   `src/i18n/en/content.json` holds Kevin's bio, facts and every card's own words; `scripts/i18n-extract.mjs` writes it
   from `src/lib/site.ts` and the synced cards (`src/i18n/source.mjs`).
2. `pnpm i18n:translate` runs the extract and then General Translation's CLI (`gt translate`, the `gt` dev dependency)
   on both files (`gt.config.json`). The CLI reads `GT_API_KEY` and `GT_PROJECT_ID` from `.env.local`, which is
   gitignored and holds the key of the GT project **kevinliu.studio** (Kevin's Random Projects organization). It writes
   `src/i18n/<locale>/{ui,content}.json` and `gt-lock.json`; commit all of them.
3. `python3 scripts/i18n-fonts.py` cuts the faces again from `~/repos/Katagami` to the characters the translations now
   print, and writes `src/i18n/fonts.json`. Commit the fonts and the file.
4. `pnpm build`. The guard fails a translated page that prints a character no face holds.

After `pnpm sync` changes a card's words, run steps 2 to 4. Until then the build prints that card's changed strings in
English and says so (`[i18n] ... changed since it was translated`), so a sync never ships a stale translation. A
translation that loses a `{placeholder}` or a link target falls back to English the same way.

## What is translated

- Every page: home, `/cards`, the 26 card pages, About, the 404, the battle's two pages; titles, descriptions,
  share-image alt text, JSON-LD (`inLanguage`, names, descriptions, breadcrumbs), the header, the footer, the skip
  links and the Machine view's records and twins.
- Every Markdown twin, and a `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` per language. A translated twin's first line names its
  language and links the English original.
- The wall's lens sheet, dock, pack and the labels and status lines `gallery.js` and `rituals.js` write: a translated
  home loads `/<code>/gallery.js` (content-addressed, immutable) between `markup.js` and `gallery.js`; it hands
  `MARKUP.sheet` the sheets in the language and rewrites the rounds' English labels as they are written, by the rules
  in `src/lib/i18n-room.ts`, which the build applies to the printed HTML too.
- The battle page's own frame: its rail and console keys, the skip hint, the log heading and every label on them.

## What stays English, and why

- **Names**: Kevin's name, product and company names, card titles, move names and trait names. Move and trait names are
  how the battle engine and its scenes know a move; a move line names its move as `{move}`, so no translation can
  rename one.
- **Receipts, code, commit SHAs, addresses, schema.org types** and the Machine view's field names (`page:`,
  `category:`), which are data.
- **The cards themselves** (the faces and their info backs). Their type is fitted to Katagami Sans's Latin advances at
  build (`markup.js` ADV, `kept()`), so translated words would overflow the printed panels; a card is a printed object,
  like a trading card from abroad. Every word on a card is on its page and its sheet in the visitor's language.
- **The game inside the battle**: turn prompts, move panels, tooltips, damage previews, the log, narration and
  cutscenes. r4/play builds hundreds of English strings inside its markup across about 850 KB of round code; they
  belong in string tables in the rounds, not in patches here. That is the next step for the battle.
- **Credits** (Cotfly's data licence) are legal attributions and keep their wording.

## The language key

A key the size of the sound and pause keys, after the nav, showing the two-letter code. It opens a list of the same
page in every language over the page, so the header never moves (CLS 0); it works without scripts (`<details>`),
closes on Escape or a press outside, and each language names itself in the system face, which holds every script.

## Faces and the phone budget

Translated pages keep the site face and add only what their language prints, declared under the same family with a
`unicode-range`, preloaded, `font-display: block` like the site face, cached a year by content address (`?v=`):
Latin 10 KB; Japanese 260 KB, Korean 63 KB, Chinese 237 KB, each fetched once per visitor. English pages declare none
of them. The Machine view's terminal face (Katagami Mono) has no CJK; CJK there falls back to the system face.
